Answer key · Lesson 2

Organelles and their jobs

Lesson plan Worksheet

  1. Write the job of each part in your own words.

    Nucleus: holds the DNA and directs making ribosomes and proteins. Ribosomes: build proteins from amino acids in the order set by mRNA. Endoplasmic reticulum: modifies proteins and makes lipids. Golgi: sorts, tags and packages proteins and lipids. Mitochondria: make ATP from the chemical energy in glucose and other nutrients. Lysosomes: digest proteins, complex sugars, lipids, nucleic acids and worn-out organelles. Cell membrane: separates the inside of the cell from its surroundings and controls what passes in and out. Cytoskeleton: holds the cell's shape, keeps some organelles in place and lets the cell and its contents move. Sources: [1, 2, 3]

  2. Put these in order to show the route of a protein that leaves the cell: Golgi apparatus, ribosome on the ER, nucleus (DNA), vesicle, endoplasmic reticulum.

    Nucleus (DNA) then ribosome on the ER then endoplasmic reticulum then vesicle then Golgi apparatus (which ships it on in new vesicles). Sources: [1, 2]

  3. Name three parts a plant cell has that an animal cell does not.

    Cell wall, chloroplasts and a large central vacuole. Sources: [1]

  4. Why does a plant wilt when it loses water?

    When water leaves, the large central vacuole shrinks, so the cells lose their firmness and the plant wilts. Sources: [1]

  5. In which direction does information travel through a neuron?

    From the dendrites, across the cell body, and down the axon. Sources: [4]

  6. A mature red blood cell has no nucleus. How does that help it do its job?

    Pushing out the nucleus and most organelles leaves more room for hemoglobin, which carries the oxygen. Sources: [5]

Exit ticket. Suppose a cell's lysosomes stopped working. Predict one problem the cell would have.

Worn-out organelles and large molecules such as proteins, complex sugars, lipids and nucleic acids would not be broken down, because lysosomes hold the enzymes that digest them. Sources: [1]
