Answer key · Lesson 7

From glucose to ATP

Lesson plan Worksheet

  1. What is ATP made of, and what happens when the last phosphate is split off?

    Adenine and the sugar ribose joined to a chain of three phosphate groups. Removing the last phosphate leaves ADP and releases energy that powers other reactions. Sources: [1]

  2. Where does glycolysis happen, and what is its net ATP gain per glucose?

    In the cytoplasm. Net gain of two ATP (four made, two used). Sources: [2]

  3. Which stage uses oxygen, and what does the oxygen become?

    The electron transport chain on the inner mitochondrial membrane. Oxygen takes the electrons and, with protons, becomes water. Sources: [3]

  4. How does ATP synthase make ATP?

    Hydrogen ions flow through it, turning parts of the machine, and that turning adds a phosphate to ADP to form ATP. Sources: [3]

  5. A mature red blood cell gets only 2 ATP from each glucose. Why? What does that tell you?

    It has no mitochondria, so it can only do glycolysis. It shows that the rest of the ATP from glucose is made in the mitochondria. Sources: [2, 3, 4]

  6. Compare the two processes.

    Cellular respiration: uses glucose and oxygen; makes carbon dioxide, water and ATP; glycolysis in the cytoplasm, the rest in mitochondria. Photosynthesis: uses light energy, carbon dioxide and water; makes sugar and oxygen; in chloroplasts. Sources: [2, 5, 3, 6, 7]

Exit ticket. Fill in the flow chart: glucose to ____ (in the cytoplasm, net ____ ATP) to pyruvate into the ____ to electron transport chain, which uses ____ and makes ____, to ATP made by ____.

Glycolysis; 2; mitochondrion (matrix); oxygen; water; ATP synthase. Sources: [2, 5, 3]
