Answer key · Lesson 5

Elements of life

Lesson plan Worksheet

  1. Count the atoms and write each formula.

    Water: 2 H, 1 O, H2O. Glucose: 6 C, 12 H, 6 O, C6H12O6. Glycine: 2 C, 5 H, 1 N, 2 O, C2H5NO2. ATP: 10 C, 16 H, 5 N, 13 O, 3 P, C10H16N5O13P3. Sources: [1, 2, 3, 4]

  2. How many of the 118 elements build all 15 molecules in the explorer? Name four of them.

    11. Any four of: hydrogen, carbon, nitrogen, oxygen, magnesium, phosphorus, sulfur, calcium, iron, zinc, selenium. Sources: [1]

  3. Which element do you need to add to carbon, hydrogen and oxygen to make an amino acid like glycine?

    Nitrogen. Sources: [3]

  4. Give one job in living things for each element.

    Carbon: links into long chains, the backbone of the molecules of life. Nitrogen: used to build DNA, RNA and amino acids. Phosphorus: forms the backbone of DNA and RNA and is part of ATP. Iron: used in hemoglobin to carry oxygen in the blood. Sources: [5]

  5. How many iron atoms are in the hemoglobin structure, and where are they?

    Four, one in each of the four heme groups (one heme per protein chain). Sources: [6, 7, 1]

Exit ticket. Write the formula for glucose. Which element would a cell need to add to glucose's elements to build glycine?

C6H12O6. Nitrogen (glycine is C2H5NO2). Sources: [2, 3]
