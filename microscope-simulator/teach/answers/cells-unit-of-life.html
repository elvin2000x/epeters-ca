Answer key · Lesson 1

Cells are the unit of life

Lesson plan Worksheet

  1. Write the three ideas of cell theory in your own words.

    1. One or more cells make up every living thing. 2. The cell is the basic unit of life. 3. New cells come from cells that already exist. Sources: [1]

  2. Fill in the table from the microscope field guide.

    Paramecium: one cell, 170 to 330 micrometres long, swims with beating cilia. Euglena: one cell, up to about 100 micrometres long, makes food with chloroplasts in light. Yeast: one cell, about 5 micrometres across, multiplies by budding. Sources: [2, 3, 4, 5]

  3. Where is the DNA in a bacterium? Where is it in an animal cell?

    In a bacterium the DNA sits in a central region called the nucleoid, because there is no nucleus. In an animal cell the DNA is inside the nucleus. Sources: [6, 7]

  4. Name two parts that every bacterium has.

    Any two of: DNA in a nucleoid, ribosomes, a cell membrane, a cell wall. Sources: [6]

  5. A red blood cell is about 8 micrometres across and a pin head is about 2 mm across. About how many red blood cells fit across a pin head? Show your work.

    2 mm = 2000 micrometres. 2000 / 8 = 250 red blood cells. Sources: [1, 8]

  6. What did you see in the pond view that supports the idea that new cells come from existing cells?

    A well-fed Paramecium splits in two (binary fission), and yeast grows a bud that pinches off. In both cases the new cell comes from a cell that already existed. Sources: [9, 10, 5]

Exit ticket. Write the three ideas of cell theory. Then give one thing you saw today that supports one of them.

One or more cells make up all living things; the cell is the basic unit of life; new cells come from existing cells. Example evidence: a Paramecium or a yeast cell is a whole living organism that is one cell, or a Paramecium dividing in two in the pond view. Sources: [1, 9, 10]
