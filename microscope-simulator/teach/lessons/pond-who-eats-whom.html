Lesson 4 · 55 min

The pond: who eats whom

Students read the field guide to find out what each pond organism eats or makes, run a feeding experiment in the pond sandbox, draw a food web and build a dichotomous key.

  • Middle school (NGSS MS)
  • Ontario Grade 9 (SNC1W)
  • Ontario Grade 11 Biology (SBI3U)
  • Ecosystems
  • Microorganisms

Student worksheet Answer key (teachers)

Learning goals

  • Sort pond organisms into those that make food from light and those that eat other organisms.
  • Draw a food web with arrows that show the direction energy flows.
  • Use population counts from the pond sandbox as evidence for how food supply affects a population.
  • Build a dichotomous key for six pond organisms.

You need

  • One device per pair
  • Student worksheet 4
  • Coloured pencils

5 min Hook

Open the pond view and drop in a few Paramecium with no food. Before pressing play, ask: what will happen to their number? Then add bacteria and ask again. Record both predictions.

  • Pond sandbox

Activity, step by step

  1. 10 min Read the field guide

    Pairs read the field guide for each organism and note what it eats or makes. Paramecium sweeps bacteria, yeast and algae into its oral groove. Amoeba engulfs bacteria and algae into a food vacuole and digests them there. Euglena makes food with its chloroplasts in light and can live on organic matter in the dark. Diatoms are single-celled algae in glass shells that make food by photosynthesis; together they make about one fifth of the world's oxygen. Sources: [1, 2, 3, 4, 5]

    • Paramecium
    • Amoeba
    • Euglena
    • Diatom

  2. 15 min Feeding experiment

    Each pair runs two slides in the pond: slide A with Paramecium and bacteria, slide B with Paramecium only. Every 10 seconds they record the Paramecium count from the 'On the slide' panel. Time runs 720 times faster than real life, so 1 second is 12 minutes. Tell students which parts are measured and which are drawn: a well-fed Paramecium dividing about every 6 hours is cited, while division timing for the other organisms is illustrative. Sources: [6, 1]

    • Pond sandbox

  3. 10 min Draw the food web

    Students draw a food web for the pond. Arrows point from the food to the eater, which is the direction energy moves. Light feeds the organisms that photosynthesise (green alga, Euglena, diatom). Bacteria, yeast and algae are eaten by Paramecium; bacteria and algae are eaten by Amoeba. Sources: [1, 2, 4, 5, 7]

  4. 10 min Build a dichotomous key

    Groups write a key with yes or no questions that ends in one name for each of the six slides (Paramecium, Amoeba, Euglena, bacteria, yeast, diatom). Useful features from the field guide: chloroplasts or golden plastids, a glass shell, a single flagellum, thousands of cilia, no fixed shape with pseudopodia, budding. Groups swap keys and test them on the microscope. Sources: [8, 9, 4, 5, 10, 11]

    • Bacteria
    • Yeast

  5. 5 min Exit ticket

    Students answer the exit ticket.

Discussion questions

  1. Which organisms in the pond could keep going with only light and water? Which could not?
  2. If all the bacteria disappeared, which organisms would be affected first, and why?
  3. The sandbox is a model. Which of its behaviours come from measurements, and which are drawn to look right? Why does that matter when we use it as evidence?

Exit ticket

Draw one food chain from the pond with at least three links, starting with light. Label which organism makes its own food.

Look for: Example: light to green alga (makes its own food by photosynthesis) to Paramecium. Or light to Euglena. Arrows go from food to eater. Sources: [1, 7, 4]

Differentiation

Support

  • Give a list of the six organisms with a picture of each and one fact already filled in.
  • Provide the food web outline with boxes placed; students only draw the arrows.
  • Press Pause in the pond while counting, then Play again.

Extension

  • Graph slide A and slide B on the same axes and write a claim, evidence and reasoning paragraph.
  • Euglena swims toward gentle light and away from very strong light. Design a pond test with the lamp spot and predict what you will see.
