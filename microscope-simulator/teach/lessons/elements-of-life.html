Lesson 5 · 45 min

Elements of life

Students find which elements build the molecules of life, write formulas from atom counts, and find out why carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus and a few metals matter to living things.

  • Middle school (NGSS MS)
  • High school (NGSS HS)
  • Ontario Grade 9 (SNC1W)
  • Ontario Grade 12 Biology (SBI4U)
  • Elements
  • Molecules

Student worksheet Answer key (teachers)

Learning goals

  • Write a chemical formula from the atoms in a molecule.
  • Name the elements that make up water, glucose, an amino acid and ATP.
  • Explain one job of carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus and iron in living things.

You need

  • One device per pair
  • Student worksheet 5

5 min Hook

Open the periodic table with Elements of life switched on. Only 11 of the 118 elements are lit, yet they build all 15 molecules in the explorer. Ask students to guess which element appears in the most atoms. Sources: [1]

  • Periodic table: Elements of life

Activity, step by step

  1. 10 min Count the atoms

    Pairs open four small molecules and record how many atoms of each element they contain, then write the formula. Water has 2 H and 1 O; glucose has 6 C, 12 H and 6 O; glycine has 2 C, 5 H, 1 N and 2 O; ATP has 10 C, 16 H, 5 N, 13 O and 3 P. Sources: [1, 2, 3, 4]

    • Water
    • Glucose
    • Glycine
    • ATP

  2. 10 min Light up a molecule on the table

    Use 'Light up a molecule' on the periodic table to show where ATP's elements sit. Then compare glucose (C, H, O) with glycine (C, H, N, O): to build an amino acid from the carbon, hydrogen and oxygen of a sugar, a cell needs another element, nitrogen. Sources: [1, 3, 5]

    • Periodic table: ATP lit up
    • Periodic table: glycine lit up

  3. 10 min What each element does

    Each pair opens one element card and reports its job in living things. Carbon atoms link into long chains, the backbone of the molecules of life. Plants take up nitrogen to build DNA, RNA and amino acids. Phosphorus forms the backbone of DNA and RNA and is part of ATP. Sulfur goes into two amino acids. Iron is used in hemoglobin to carry oxygen. Magnesium sits at the centre of chlorophyll. Zinc sits at the active site of many enzymes. Calcium phosphate is the main mineral in bone. Sources: [6]

    • Carbon
    • Nitrogen
    • Phosphorus
    • Sulfur
    • Iron
    • Magnesium
    • Zinc
    • Calcium

  4. 5 min Iron in a big molecule

    Open hemoglobin with only its iron atoms lit. The structure has four heme groups, each holding one iron atom. Point out a data caveat: big X-ray structure files list no hydrogen atoms, so their atom counts are lower than the real molecule. Sources: [7, 8, 1]

    • Hemoglobin: iron lit up

  5. 5 min Exit ticket

    Students answer the exit ticket.

Discussion questions

  1. Carbon is in every molecule of life in the explorer except water. What property of carbon atoms makes that possible?
  2. Why might a plant that cannot get nitrogen from the soil grow poorly?
  3. The hemoglobin file shows no hydrogen at all. How should we use atom counts from a file like that?

Exit ticket

Write the formula for glucose. Which element would a cell need to add to glucose's elements to build glycine?

Look for: C6H12O6. Nitrogen (glycine is C2H5NO2). Sources: [2, 3]

Differentiation

Support

  • Give a formula frame: C__ H__ O__ N__ P__ for students to fill in.
  • Show how to read the small numbers on lit elements before pairs start.
  • Pair element cards so each student reads one short description.

Extension

  • Which of the 15 molecules has the most kinds of elements? Use the periodic table's light-up menu to find out.
  • Palmitic acid is C16H32O2. Use its formula to explain why it has no nitrogen or phosphorus to offer a cell building DNA.
