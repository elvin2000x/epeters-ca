Lesson 7 · 60 min

From glucose to ATP

Students follow the energy in a glucose molecule into ATP: glycolysis in the cytoplasm, then the mitochondrion and ATP synthase, solve the red blood cell puzzle, and compare respiration with photosynthesis.

  • Middle school (NGSS MS)
  • High school (NGSS HS)
  • Ontario Grade 9 (SNC1W)
  • Ontario Grade 12 Biology (SBI4U)
  • Energy
  • Cells
  • Molecules

Student worksheet Answer key (teachers)

Learning goals

  • Describe how ATP stores and releases energy.
  • Place glycolysis in the cytoplasm and the oxygen-using stage in the mitochondrion.
  • Explain why a mature red blood cell gets only two ATP from each glucose.
  • Compare the inputs and outputs of cellular respiration and photosynthesis.

You need

  • One device per pair
  • Student worksheet 7

5 min Hook

Open the muscle cell's mitochondria. Muscle cells have a very high concentration of mitochondria, which make the ATP the cell needs. Ask: where does the energy to lift your arm actually come from, and how does it get to the muscle? Sources: [10]

  • Muscle cell: mitochondria

Activity, step by step

  1. 10 min Two molecules

    Open glucose and then ATP. Glucose is a six-carbon sugar, C6H12O6, and cells break it down in cellular respiration to make ATP. ATP is adenine and the sugar ribose joined to a chain of three phosphates. Splitting off the last phosphate leaves ADP and releases energy that powers other reactions. Sources: [1, 2, 3]

    • Glucose
    • ATP

  2. 15 min The route, stage by stage

    Glycolysis happens in the cytoplasm and does not use oxygen directly: one glucose becomes two pyruvate, making four ATP but using two, a net gain of two ATP (plus two NADH). Pyruvate moves into the mitochondrial matrix, where its carbons leave as carbon dioxide. On the inner membrane the electron transport chain, the only stage that uses oxygen, hands electrons to oxygen, which with protons forms water. Hydrogen ions flow through ATP synthase, and its turning parts add a phosphate to ADP to make ATP. Zoom the home journey from the mitochondrion (about 2 micrometres long) to ATP synthase (head about 9 nanometres) to one ATP. Sources: [4, 5, 6, 7, 8]

    • Home zoom: mitochondrion
    • Home zoom: ATP synthase
    • Home zoom: ATP

  3. 10 min The red blood cell puzzle

    Open the red blood cell. A mature one has no nucleus and few other organelles. Mature mammalian red blood cells have no mitochondria, so they harvest only two ATP from each glucose. Ask pairs: what does that tell you about where the rest of glucose's ATP is made? Sources: [9, 4]

    • Red blood cell

  4. 12 min The other half of the cycle

    Open the plant cell's chloroplast. Chloroplasts use light energy, carbon dioxide and water to make sugar, and release oxygen. Students fill a two-column table: what respiration uses and makes, and what photosynthesis uses and makes. Notice that each process makes what the other uses. Sources: [10, 11, 5, 6]

    • Plant cell: chloroplast
    • Plant cell: mitochondria

  5. 8 min Exit ticket

    Students complete the flow chart exit ticket.

Discussion questions

  1. Plant cells have both chloroplasts and mitochondria. Why would a plant need both?
  2. Where does the oxygen you breathe in end up, according to today's route?
  3. Why do you think the number of ATP made from one glucose is given as a range rather than one exact number?

Exit ticket

Fill in the flow chart: glucose to ____ (in the cytoplasm, net ____ ATP) to pyruvate into the ____ to electron transport chain, which uses ____ and makes ____, to ATP made by ____.

Look for: Glycolysis; 2; mitochondrion (matrix); oxygen; water; ATP synthase. Sources: [4, 5, 6]

Differentiation

Support

  • Give the flow chart with arrows and boxes drawn, and a word bank.
  • Stop the home zoom at each level and read the fact line aloud.
  • Use 'energy currency' as a hook: ATP is spent, ADP is the empty wallet that gets refilled.

Extension

  • OpenStax says the ATP yield per glucose varies. Find two reasons it gives and explain one.
  • ATP has 47 atoms, 3 of them phosphorus. Which part of the molecule holds all three, and how does that connect to splitting off a phosphate to release energy?

    Construct a scientific explanation based on evidence for the role of photosynthesis in the cycling of matter and flow of energy into and out of organisms.
  • MS-LS1-7 Middle school (NGSS MS)
    Develop a model to describe how food is rearranged through chemical reactions forming new molecules that support growth and/or release energy as this matter moves through an organism.
  • HS-LS1-7 High school (NGSS HS)
    Use a model to illustrate that cellular respiration is a chemical process whereby the bonds of food molecules and oxygen molecules are broken and the bonds in new compounds are formed, resulting in a net transfer of energy.
  • B2.3 Ontario Grade 9 (SNC1W)
    compare and contrast the processes of cellular respiration and photosynthesis, and explain how their complementary relationship contributes to the dynamic equilibrium of ecosystems
  • C2.1 Ontario Grade 12 Biology (SBI4U)
    use appropriate terminology related to metabolism, including, but not limited to: energy carriers, glycolysis, Krebs cycle, electron transport chain, ATP synthase, oxidative phosphorylation, chemiosmosis, proton pump, photolysis, Calvin cycle, light and dark reactions, and cyclic and noncyclic phosphorylation
  • C3.1 Ontario Grade 12 Biology (SBI4U)
    explain the chemical changes and energy conversions associated with the processes of aerobic and anaerobic cellular respiration (e.g., in aerobic cellular respiration, glucose and oxygen react to produce carbon dioxide, water, and energy in the form of heat and ATP; in anaerobic cellular respiration, yeast reacts with glucose in the absence of oxygen to produce carbon dioxide and ethanol)

    describe, compare, and illustrate (e.g., using flow charts) the matter and energy transformations that occur during the processes of cellular respiration (aerobic and anaerobic) and photosynthesis, including the roles of oxygen and organelles such as mitochondria and chloroplasts
