Sources

Every science fact in the lessons, worksheets and answer keys names one of these sources. Facts are written in our own words.

  • OpenStax Biology 2e, section 4.1 Studying Cells.
    A cell is the smallest unit of a living thing; cell theory (Schleiden and Schwann, late 1830s; Virchow later contributed): one or more cells make up all living things, the cell is the basic unit of life, new cells arise from existing cells. A typical human red blood cell is about 8 micrometres across; a pin head is about 2 mm across, so about 250 red blood cells fit across it. Student light microscopes magnify up to about 400 times, about 1,000 times with oil immersion, resolution about 200 nanometres; electron microscopes about 100,000 times. Several interconnected cells of one kind with a shared function form a tissue. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 1, 3.
  • OpenStax Biology 2e, section 7.2 Glycolysis.
    Glycolysis happens in the cytoplasm of prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells and does not use oxygen directly. It starts with glucose and makes two pyruvate, four ATP and two NADH; two ATP are used in the first half, so the net gain is two ATP. Mature mammalian red blood cells have no mitochondria, so they harvest only two ATP from each glucose. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 7.
  • OpenStax Biology 2e, section 7.3 Oxidation of Pyruvate and the Citric Acid Cycle.
    Pyruvate enters the mitochondrial matrix and loses a carbon as carbon dioxide; carbon dioxide is one of the major end products of cellular respiration. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 7.
  • OpenStax Biology 2e, section 7.4 Oxidative Phosphorylation.
    The electron transport chain sits in the inner mitochondrial membrane and is the only part of glucose metabolism that uses oxygen; electrons reduce oxygen and, with protons, make water. Hydrogen ions pass through ATP synthase, and the turning of parts of this machine adds a phosphate to ADP to make ATP. The number of ATP made per glucose varies. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 7.
  • OpenStax Biology 2e, section 8.1 Overview of Photosynthesis.
    Photosynthesis uses solar energy, carbon dioxide and water to make energy-storing carbohydrates, and releases oxygen as a waste product; it is the only biological process that captures energy from sunlight into chemical compounds. Plants, algae and cyanobacteria are the organisms that photosynthesise; because they use light to make their own food they are called photoautotrophs. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 4, 7.
  • OpenStax Biology 2e, section 15.1 The Genetic Code.
    Transcription makes messenger RNA (mRNA) with the letters A, C, G and U; translation on ribosomes turns it into protein. Each amino acid is set by a three-nucleotide codon; there are 16 possible two-letter and 64 possible three-letter combinations. AUG codes for methionine and is the start codon; after it the mRNA is read in threes until a stop codon. Inserting one or two nucleotides shifts the reading frame and changes every later amino acid (Crick and Brenner). Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 8.
  • OpenStax Biology 2e, section 15.2 Prokaryotic Transcription.
    The mRNA is complementary to the DNA template strand and matches the coding strand except that every T is replaced by U. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 8.
  • OpenStax Biology 2e, section 15.5 Ribosomes and Protein Synthesis.
    Translation needs an mRNA template, ribosomes, tRNAs and enzyme factors. Translation ends at a nonsense (stop) codon: UAA, UAG or UGA. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 8.
  • OpenStax Chemistry 2e, section 2.3 Atomic Structure and Symbolism.
    Protons (charge 1+, mass about 1.0073 amu) and neutrons (charge 0, mass about 1.0087 amu) are in the nucleus; electrons (charge 1-, about 0.00055 amu) are outside it, and about 1800 electrons equal the mass of one proton. The number of protons is the atomic number and sets the element; a neutral atom has as many electrons as protons. An atom is on the order of 10 to the minus 10 metres across and its nucleus about 100,000 times smaller. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 6.
  • NIST Office of Weights and Measures, Metric (SI) Prefixes.
    milli = one thousandth (10 to the minus 3), micro = one millionth (10 to the minus 6), nano = one billionth (10 to the minus 9). Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 1, 3.
  • OpenStax Biology 2e, section 4.2 Prokaryotic Cells.
    Prokaryotes have no nucleus and no other membrane-bound organelles; every prokaryote has DNA in a nucleoid, ribosomes, a cell membrane and a cell wall; some have flagella; most bacteria have a peptidoglycan wall. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 1.
  • OpenStax Biology 2e, section 4.3 Eukaryotic Cells.
    Jobs of the membrane, nucleus, mitochondria, ribosomes, lysosomes, chloroplasts, cell wall and central vacuole; plant cells add a wall, chloroplasts and a large central vacuole and mostly lack lysosomes; muscle cells have a very high concentration of mitochondria. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 1, 2, 7, 8.
  • OpenStax Biology 2e, section 4.4 The Endomembrane System and Proteins.
    The endoplasmic reticulum modifies proteins and makes lipids (rough ER has ribosomes); the Golgi sorts, tags and packages proteins and lipids that arrive in vesicles from the ER; in plants the Golgi also makes complex sugars for the wall. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 2, 8.
  • OpenStax Biology 2e, section 4.5 The Cytoskeleton.
    The cytoskeleton holds the cell's shape and lets the cell and its contents move; microfilaments are two twisted strands of actin. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 2.
  • OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, section 10.2 Skeletal Muscle.
    Skeletal muscle cells (fibres) can be up to about 30 cm long and have many nuclei, formed by fusion of many cells. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 1.
  • OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, section 12.2 Nervous Tissue.
    Information flows from the dendrites, across the cell body and down the axon; myelin is made by glial cells. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 2.
  • OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, section 18.3 Erythrocytes.
    Red blood cells carry oxygen held by hemoglobin; a mature one has no nucleus and few organelles; each hemoglobin carries up to four oxygen molecules. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 2, 7.
  • OpenStax Biology 2e, section 3.2 Carbohydrates.
    Glucose is C6H12O6; cellular respiration releases energy from glucose to make ATP. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 7.
  • OpenStax Biology 2e, section 3.4 Proteins.
    20 common amino acids; glycine's R group is a hydrogen atom; peptide bonds join amino acids; insulin helps regulate blood glucose; actin and myosin cause muscle contraction. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 5.
  • OpenStax Biology 2e, section 3.5 Nucleic Acids.
    DNA is the genetic material; double helix; sugar and phosphate backbone outside; A pairs with T and G with C. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 8.
  • OpenStax Biology 2e, section 6.4 ATP: Adenosine Triphosphate.
    ATP is the cell's main energy currency: adenine and ribose bound to three phosphates; removing the last phosphate gives ADP and releases energy. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 7.

    Formula C10 H16 N5 O13 P3. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 5.

    Formula C6 H12 O6. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 5, 7.
  • RCSB PDB Chemical Component Dictionary, GLY (glycine).
    Formula C2 H5 N O2. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 5.
  • RCSB PDB 1BNA, Structure of a B-DNA dodecamer.
    Two copies of the DNA strand CGCGAATTCGCG (12 nucleotides each). Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 8.
  • RCSB PDB 4HHB, human deoxyhaemoglobin.
    Two alpha and two beta chains; four heme groups, each with iron. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 5.
  • RCSB PDB 4INS, 2Zn pig insulin.
    Chain A has 21 amino acids and chain B has 30. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 8.
  • RCSB PDB 1LYZ, hen egg-white lysozyme.
    One chain of 129 amino acids. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 8.
  • Ormo, M. et al. (1996). Crystal structure of the Aequorea victoria green fluorescent protein. Science 273, 1392-1395.
    GFP is a marker for gene expression; its chromophore forms by itself from three amino acids at the centre of an 11-strand barrel. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 8.
  • OpenStax Biology 2e, section 40.2 Components of the Blood.
    Hemoglobin carries oxygen, is packed into red blood cells, and has four subunits each with an iron-containing heme. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 5.
  • Neupane, L. Paramecium caudatum: Habitat, Culture, Structure, Locomotion. Microbe Notes.
    170 to 330 micrometres long; 10,000 to 14,000 cilia. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 1, 3, 4.
  • Brette, R. (2021). Integrative Neuroscience of Paramecium, a Swimming Neuron. eNeuro 8(3).
    Avoiding reaction; feeds on bacteria, yeast and algae swept into the oral groove; a well-fed Paramecium can divide every 6 hours. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 1, 4.
  • Microbiological Garden, Amoebae: food uptake.
    Amoebae are predators that engulf bacteria and algae into a food vacuole. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 4.
  • Microbe Notes, Amoeba proteus: Habitat, Culture and Structures.
    Pseudopodia move the amoeba and engulf prey; food is digested inside the food vacuole. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 4.
  • Kreutz, M. Amoeba proteus. Real Micro Life.
    160 to 760 micrometres long; one nucleus 22 to 62 micrometres across. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 4.
  • Haeder, D.-P. and Hemmersbach, R. (2022). Euglena, a Gravitactic Flagellate of Multiple Usages. Life 12.
    About 100 micrometres long; red eyespot near the flagellum; chloroplasts; can live on organic material in the dark; swims toward weak light and away from strong light; divides lengthwise. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 1, 4.
  • Freshwater Biodiversity Portal of Bangladesh, Euglena gracilis Klebs, 1883.
    Up to 100 micrometres long, can round up to about 20 micrometres; photosynthesis and phagocytosis. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 1.
  • Kaiser, G. 2.1 Sizes, Shapes, and Arrangements of Bacteria. Microbiology (LibreTexts), CC BY 4.0.
    Three basic shapes; average rod 1 to 4 micrometres long; average sphere 0.5 to 1 micrometre. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 3, 4.
  • BioNumbers BNID 101796, diameter of budding yeast.
    Saccharomyces cerevisiae is about 5 micrometres across. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 1.
  • BioNumbers BNID 103716, daughter to mother volume at first budding.
    Daughter cell reaches 0.5 to 0.7 of the mother's volume at the first budding. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 1, 4.
  • University of Exeter News (2025). The Mystery of Diatom Movement Uncovered in New Study.
    Diatoms are single-celled photosynthetic algae in glass shells, glide using the raphe, and make about one fifth of the world's oxygen. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 4.
  • Microbe Notes, Binary Fission: Steps, Types, Examples.
    Many bacteria and some eukaryotes such as amoeba and Paramecium reproduce by binary fission; daughters carry the same genetic material. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 1.
  • Microscope World, How to Calculate Microscope Field of View.
    Field of view diameter = eyepiece field number divided by objective magnification; field number 18 with a 10x objective gives 1.8 mm. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 3.
  • Milo and Phillips, Cell Biology by the Numbers: How big are mitochondria?
    Textbook mitochondria are about 2 micrometres long and 1 micrometre across. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 7.
  • Boundless Microbiology (LibreTexts), 5.5C F1F0 ATP Synthase.
    F1 particles of 9 nm diameter dot the inner mitochondrial membrane. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 3, 7.
  • Royal Society of Chemistry, Periodic Table, one page per element.
    Element descriptions (for example carbon chains, nitrogen in DNA and amino acids, phosphorus in DNA and ATP, iron in haemoglobin, magnesium in chlorophyll, zinc in enzymes), electron configurations and most abundant isotopes. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 5, 6, 8.
  • National Library of Medicine, PubChem Periodic Table of Elements.
    Element category and first ionization energy (eV). Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 6.
  • Cell and Molecule Explorer, web/data/links.json (atoms counted from each structure file by tools/build_links.py).
    Atom counts per element for each of the 15 molecules; 11 elements across the 15 molecules; big X-ray structures list no hydrogen atoms. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 5.

    The zoom starts with a view 0.6 mm across and ends at 0.4 nanometres (4.0 x 10 to the minus 10 m); each level cites its own source. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 1, 3, 6.

Paramecium division (6 hours) and its swimming and bacterial runs are cited; other organisms' division timing and crawling speeds are illustrative. Read 2026-10-01. Used by: 3, 4.
